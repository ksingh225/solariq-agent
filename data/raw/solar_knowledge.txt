Solar Photovoltaic System Fundamentals

Solar photovoltaic systems convert sunlight into electrical energy using photovoltaic modules.

Solar irradiance is the power of solar radiation received per unit area and is commonly expressed in watts per square metre (W/m²).

Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI) represents the total solar radiation received on a horizontal surface.

Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI) represents the amount of solar radiation received directly from the direction of the sun.

Diffuse Horizontal Irradiance (DHI) represents solar radiation that reaches a surface after being scattered by the atmosphere.

Cloud cover can significantly reduce solar irradiance reaching photovoltaic modules and therefore reduce expected energy generation.

Photovoltaic system performance is affected by irradiance, temperature, shading, soiling, equipment efficiency, inverter performance and system availability.

A simplified estimate of photovoltaic generation can be calculated using installed capacity, an irradiance factor and an assumed system efficiency.

For operational monitoring, measured generation should be compared with expected generation to identify performance deviations.

A significant deviation between expected and actual generation may require investigation of weather conditions, shading, soiling, inverter status, equipment availability or other operational factors.

AI-based decision support should distinguish between measured observations, calculated values and inferred causes. Recommendations involving operational risk should be subject to appropriate human review.
